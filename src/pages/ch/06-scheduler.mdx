<summary>The scheduler's arithmetic, once</summary>

A prefill batch of $P$ tokens takes about $P \cdot t_{\text{tok}}$; a decode step takes $t_{\text{step}} \approx t_{\text{mem}}$. With prefill-first, a newcomer gets $\text{TTFT} \approx P\,t_{\text{tok}}$ and everyone else sees a gap of that plus one decode step. Capping prefill batches at $c$ tokens and alternating gives

$$
\text{gap} \approx c\,t_{\text{tok}} + t_{\text{step}}, \qquad \text{TTFT} \approx \lceil P/c \rceil \cdot c\,t_{\text{tok}} + (\lceil P/c \rceil - 1)\, t_{\text{step}}
$$

Mixing a chunk of $c \le n^* - B$ into each decode step removes the gap entirely and gives $\text{TTFT} \approx \lceil P/c \rceil \cdot t_{\text{step}}$.

In SGLang, `schedule_policy` orders the waiting list, and the loop above is `Scheduler.get_next_batch_to_run()`, which tries to build a prefill batch first and falls back to the running decode batch. `chunked_prefill_size` caps a prefill batch and is on by default (8,192 tokens on an H100), but a prompt that is partway through its chunks gets its next chunk on the very next step, so on its own it does not shorten the freeze. `--prefill-decode-interval N` runs $N$ decode steps after every prefill batch, and $N = 1$ gives the alternation in step 6. `--enable-mixed-chunk` turns on mixing; the chunk and the decodes then share one budget, so $c$ = `chunked_prefill_size` $- B$.
