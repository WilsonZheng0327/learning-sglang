<summary>The prefix arithmetic, once</summary>

Position $i$'s key and value are functions of tokens $1..i$, so two requests can share cache exactly for their longest common prefix $m$ and nothing past it. A shared prefix of $s$ tokens across $r$ requests costs $s$ tokens of cache instead of $r \cdot s$, and a newcomer with an $L$-token prompt prefills $L - m$ tokens:

$$
\text{TTFT} \approx \max\!\left(t_{\text{mem}},\; (L - m)\,t_{\text{tok}}\right)
$$

In a chat of $T$ turns with question $q_t$ and answer $a_t$, prefill without caching sums to $\sum_t (q_t + \sum_{u<t}(q_u + a_u))$ and with it to $\sum_t q_t$. SGLang's `RadixCache` is the tree, reference counts pin the pages of running requests, eviction is least-recently-used over unreferenced nodes, and `--schedule-policy lpm` orders the waiting list by matched length. The default policy is `fcfs`, and lpm itself falls back to it past 128 waiting requests; a newcomer's prefix is matched when it is admitted under any policy. Matching works in whole pages, so with page size $p$ up to $p - 1$ tokens at a boundary are prefilled again, and an answer's last token always is, because its k and v were never written.
