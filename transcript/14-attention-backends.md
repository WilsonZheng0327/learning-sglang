## Prose · The arithmetic, and where the kernels come from

**What attention reads.** Llama-3-8B keeps, per token per layer, 8 K/V heads × 128 numbers × (k and v) × 2 bytes = 4 KB, and 32 layers make 128 KB per token (chapter 3). A decode step reads every running request's whole cache once, so the attention kernels together move $B \times n \times 128$ KB, against a fixed 16 GB of weights. At $B = 256$ the two are equal at $n \approx 480$; at $n = 4096$ the cache read is 134 GB, and at 3.35 TB/s that step takes 45 ms instead of 4.8.

**Why it is a memory pump.** For one query against one key in one head: a 128-wide dot product is 256 flops and reads 256 bytes of $k$; scaling and adding a 128-wide $v$ is another 256 flops and 256 bytes. One flop per byte. The H100's ratio of compute to bandwidth is $989 \cdot 10^{12} / 3.35 \cdot 10^{12} \approx 295$ flops per byte (chapter 4), so a single-query attention sits about 300× below the balance point. Grouped-query attention lifts it to 4 (four query heads per K/V head); prefill lifts it by the height of the query tile, 64 to 128, which is why prefill attention is compute-bound and decode attention never is.

**FlashAttention.** Written as three kernels, attention materialises the $n \times n$ score matrix per head in HBM: at a 2,000-token prefill that is 8 MB per head, written and read back twice (scores, then weights), 33 MB of traffic against the 1 MB of K and V that actually carry information. FlashAttention (Dao, Fu, Ermon, Rudra, Ré, 2022) computes the softmax *online*: one kernel walks K and V in chunks, keeps a running result, and never writes the scores out, so the traffic is K and V once. How the running result stays exactly equal to the full softmax is FlashAttention's contribution and is not covered here. FlashAttention-2 (Dao, 2023) reorganised the loop so a thread block owns a tile of queries and sweeps all keys, which suits prefill. Flash-Decoding (Dao, Haziza, Massa, Sizov, 2023) splits the keys across blocks for decode and merges their partial results. FlashAttention-3 (Shah, Bikshandi, Zhang, Thakkar, Ramani, Dao, 2024) rewrote it for Hopper: producer warps drive TMA loads, two consumer warpgroups alternate so one's softmax overlaps the other's matmul, and FP8 with per-block scaling; it reaches about 75% of the H100's peak where FA2 reached about 35%.

**Paged, ragged batches.** SGLang's pool is page size 1 (chapter 7), so a request's K/V is a list of page numbers in token order. The batch is described to the kernel by `kv_indptr` (where each request's slice starts) and `kv_indices` (the concatenated page numbers), a CSR layout; backends with larger pages add `kv_last_page_len`. The arrays are rebuilt on the CPU each batch in `init_forward_metadata`; FlashInfer (Ye et al., 2025) calls this `plan()`, and uses it to choose the split-KV chunking per request and balance long and short contexts across SMs. For decode the arrays live in buffers sized for the largest captured batch, which is how chapter 11's graph can replay a kernel whose inputs change every step.

**The interface.** `sglang/srt/layers/attention/base_attn_backend.py` defines `AttentionBackend`: `init_forward_metadata(forward_batch)`, `forward_extend(q, k, v, layer, forward_batch)`, `forward_decode(...)`, `init_cuda_graph_state(max_bs, ...)`, and the capture/replay hooks that refresh the captured buffers. `RadixAttention.forward` in `radix_attention.py` does nothing but call `attn_backend.forward(q, k, v, self, forward_batch)`. `attention_registry.py` maps `--attention-backend` names to classes; the docs' feature table (docs.sglang.io, *Attention Backend*) lists per backend the page sizes, cache dtypes, sliding-window and speculative-decoding support. Defaults: `fa3` on Hopper, `flashinfer` on other NVIDIA (falling back to `triton`), `trtllm_mha` / `trtllm_mla` on Blackwell. `--prefill-attention-backend` and `--decode-attention-backend` mix two.

**MLA.** DeepSeek-V2/V3 cache one 576-wide vector per token (a 512-wide latent plus 64 rope dimensions) shared by all 128 heads, and un-project it per head inside the kernel. The kernel's head widths (576 in, 512 out) match no MHA kernel, so MLA has its own family: `flashinfer` (MLA mode), `flashmla`, `cutlass_mla`, `trtllm_mla`, and `fa3` / `fa4` for MLA shapes.
